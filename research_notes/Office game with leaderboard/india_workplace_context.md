# Indian Workplace Context for an Office Game (small Indian tech/IT services company with interns)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Method note: research was done via web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (hofstede-insights.com and scconline.com failed DNS resolution), so some figures rely on search-result summaries of the pages cited. ## 1. Indian workplace culture relevant to games and public recognition (hierarchy, collectivism, face-saving, public ranking, interns vs seniors, gender, language)

### Takeaway
India is a high power-distance culture (Hofstede PDI 77) with mixed individualism/collectivism (IDV 48) and very strong in-group (team/family) loyalty per GLOBE. Recognition is a top motivator for Indian professionals, but upward voice is culturally discouraged and juniors are under-recognised. Public praise works best when it is endorsed by seniors and given in a psychologically safe setting, and team-level glory often lands better than exposing individuals at the bottom of a ranking.

### Cited Findings
**Hofstede / GLOBE**
- India's Hofstede Power Distance score is 77 (ranked about 10th/11th of the countries evaluated), and its Individualism score is 48. Both come from Hofstede's original IBM-era data. — [arXiv: E-banking and E-commerce in India and USA (Table 1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.2741); [LibreTexts: Hofstede's Dimensions](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Communication/Intercultural_Communication/Intercultural_Communication_%28Ahrndt%29/03:_Beliefs_Values_and_Cultural_Universals/3.02:_Hofstede%27s_Dimensions_of_Culture_Theory)
- Hofstede Insights describes India's IDV of 48 as "a society with both collectivistic and individualist traits". — [Medium summary of Hofstede Insights India page](https://medium.com/@kumarsaurav.031/dimensions-of-indian-culture-from-hofstede-insights-a46d23b5acc1)
- Hofstede Insights now operates as "The Culture Factor" (rebranded around 2023). I could not confirm whether India's scores were revised in that update. — [Komisarof & Akaliyski, Keio](https://www.k-ris.keio.ac.jp/html/publish_file/100000064/11994104_pdf_input_1.pdf)
- GLOBE's Southern Asia cluster, which includes India, has "high cultural practice scores on the dimensions of In-Group Collectivism, Power Distance, and Humane Orientation". Performance Orientation, Future Orientation, Uncertainty Avoidance and Institutional Collectivism fall in the middle range. — [GLOBE Project: Southern Asia cluster](https://globeproject.com/results/clusters/southern-asia%3Fmenu=list.html)
- GLOBE defines In-Group Collectivism as "the degree to which individuals express (and should express) pride, loyalty, and cohesiveness in their organizations or families". — [GLOBE Project](https://globeproject.com/study_2004_2007)

**Voice, hierarchy and face-saving**
- An Emerald paper on employee voice frames India as a context "where upward communication is culturally discouraged". — [Subhakaran & Dyaram, IJPPM](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/doi/abs/10.1108/IJPPM-10-2017-0276?af=R)
- Prince & Rao (2020) surveyed 254 executives in Indian IT companies on promotive and prohibitive voice and managerial openness. — [IJOA: Voice behavior of Indian IT employees](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJOA-11-2019-1926/full/html)
- A 2026 RGIPT extended abstract found that an unfavourable interpersonal climate and emotional strain best explain employee silence in Indian organisations. — [RGIPT abstract](https://www.rgipt.ac.in/site/writereaddata/UploadNews/pdf/C_202605061413120995.pdf)
- SHRM India commentary makes three points: India sits among the higher Power Distance scorers; in high-PD settings, publicly pointing out a superior's mistake can feel disrespectful; and "not losing face" discourages admitting ignorance. This is commentary, not a study. — [SHRM India: Psychological safety in India](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/blogs/why-psychological-safety-in-india-requires-more-than-an-open-door-policy); [SHRM India: Why employees stay silent](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/blogs/why-employees-stay-silent-leaders-encourage-openness)

**Recognition in Indian workplaces**
- About 92% of Indian professionals say workplace recognition is their strongest motivating factor (Avtar Group & EY GDS, "Why We Work", Nov 2025). — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/socialstory/2025/11/recognition-at-workplace-strongest-motivation-factor-study-report); [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/employee-engagement/recognition-beats-money-as-key-motivator-for-92percent-of-professionals-report-47165)
- Only 55% of employees in India feel truly recognised. Non-managerial employees and new hires are "significantly under-recognised". In workplaces without psychological safety, public praise can feel uncomfortable or inauthentic. (Great Place To Work India x Vantage Circle, "The Recognition Effect", 2025.) — [GPTW India PDF](https://www.greatplacetowork.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/the-recognition-effect-great-place-to-work-x-vantage-circle-1.pdf); [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/benefits-and-rewards/only-55-of-employees-feel-truly-recognised-at-work-new-study-reveals-46043)
- 63% of Indian employees rate recognition from senior leadership as one of the most critical factors in a positive workplace culture (Indeed). — [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/indian-employees-crave-recognition-and-openness-at-work-indeed-finds/)
- Engagement is low. Gallup puts India's engaged employees at 23% in 2025, the lowest in four years. ADP Research reports India workforce engagement falling to 19% in 2025. The two surveys use different methods. — [Gallup: Rebuilding Indian Workplaces](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713372/rebuilding-indian-workplaces-for-the-future-report.aspx); [ADP India](https://in.adp.com/about-adp/press-centre/india-workforce-engagement-plummets-to-19-percent-in-2025.aspx)
- TCS has used gamification and a custom social platform explicitly to encourage interaction across job levels ("breaks hierarchical structures"). — [HRD Connect](https://www.hrdconnect.com/?p=59999); [People Matters: Practicing what is preached](https://www.peoplematters.in/?p=10430)

**Gender**
- Women are about 36% of India's tech workforce: over 2 million of roughly 5.03 million, up from about 900,000 in FY13 (Nasscom). They are under-represented in specialist roles: under 12% in AI/data science and 6% in cybersecurity. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/business%2Feconomy%2F36-of-it-workforce-are-women-data-doubles-in-a-decade-2656362); [Nasscom Women & IT Scorecard](https://nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/women-and-it-scorecard-india)
- Policy commentary notes that long hours and night shifts extend the "workplace" beyond office premises, and that safety obligations follow. State rules on women working night shifts carry safety conditions. — [PolicyCircle](https://www.policycircle.org/industry/it-sector-gender-imbalance/); [IndBiz](https://indbiz.gov.in/indias-it-sector-hired-over-1-million-women-since-fy23)

### Inferences
- With high power distance, the game needs visible endorsement from founders and leads. Having seniors play too, and lose sometimes, signals that it is safe for interns to compete. Seniors being "beaten" by interns should be framed as fun, not as loss of face.
- Ranking individuals at the bottom is risky in a face-conscious culture. Safer designs include: top-N or tier-based display rather than a full rank list; team (in-group) leaderboards; "most improved" categories; and private personal stats.
- Strong in-group collectivism suggests team or "franchise" competition (IPL-style squads) will motivate more than a pure individual race. Mixing interns and seniors in squads builds cross-level ties, which TCS also targeted.
- Upward voice is discouraged, so mechanics that reward juniors for asking questions, flagging bugs, or giving peer kudos to seniors can legitimise speaking up. Anonymous or low-stakes channels help.
- Gender: keep events in working hours and avoid late-evening in-person-only finales. Offer remote-friendly participation. Avoid mechanics that reward after-hours activity, which disadvantages those with caregiving or safety constraints.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the current Culture Factor/Hofstede Insights India page (fetch blocked), so I could not confirm whether the 77/48 scores, or the other dimensions (Motivation 56, UAI 40, LTO 51, Indulgence 26, as commonly cited), were revised. Verify before quoting the other dimensions.
- I found no official GLOBE India country scores (only the cluster description).
- I found no Indian study directly measuring preference for public vs private recognition or comfort with public ranking.
- Language (English/Tamil/Hindi mix): I found no study on language in Indian workplace games. Any recommendation on bilingual content is inference only (see Section 6).

## 2. Gamified learning/engagement programs in Indian IT companies and reported results

### Takeaway
The large Indian IT firms (Infosys, TCS, Wipro, HCL) have all used points, badges, leaderboards and challenge platforms for learning and onboarding. Published "results" are almost all vendor or self-reported. The clearest transferable pattern is Wipro TopGear's use of real projects as challenges with rewards, which maps directly onto "ties to real work".

### Cited Findings
- **Infosys Wingspan:** learners "earn cool 'Badges' and climb the leaderboard with every course they complete". Infosys says leaderboards and badges "boost motivation and create a sense of accomplishment". Wingspan won a Brandon Hall Gold (2023) and Stevie awards. — [Infosys Wingspan overview](https://www.infosys.com/products-and-platforms/wingspan/overview.html); [Infosys newsroom 2023](https://infosys.com/newsroom/features/2023/wins-gold-excellence-technology-awards.html); [Stevie Awards](https://stevieawards.com/node/17558)
- **Infosys Enterprise Gamification Platform:** in a service-desk case study, a visual leaderboard plus performance rewards cut mean time to resolve tickets (MTTR) by 10%. This is a vendor case study on an operational metric, not a learning metric. — [Infosys BPM case study](https://www.infosysbpm.com/offerings/industries/edutech-services/case-studies/documents/hitting-it-out-of-the-park-by-gamification.pdf)
- I found no independent or peer-reviewed evidence of learning outcomes for Infosys Lex/Wingspan. — (absence noted from search of Infosys materials above)
- **TCS:** entry-level onboarding was gamified through the Xplore digital learning program, with the onboarding sequence depending on each person's score. TCS also used a custom social platform to drive interaction across levels. — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/?p=10430); [HRD Connect](https://www.hrdconnect.com/?p=59999)
- **Wipro TopGear:** "real projects are presented as challenges to employees where they can compete and win cash rewards". — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/?p=10430)
- **HCL:** used game elements in hiring and onboarding; more than 60% of candidates actively played (2013 report). A theme-park scenario game with a points leaderboard is also described, though that source does not clearly name HCL. — [Social Samosa 2014](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2014/01/gamification-indian-organizations-examples); [People Matters: Using gamification for employee engagement](https://www.peoplematters.in/article/lnd-week/using-gamification-employee-engagement-12387)
- **Tata Group** has gamified hiring. — [Tata.com](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/careers/gamifying-hiring-tata-group)
- Nasscom community posts collect "real stories" of making online learning more engaging, including through gamification. — [Nasscom Community](https://community.nasscom.in/communities/talent/6-ways-to-make-online-learning-more-engaging-some-real-stories.html)

### Inferences
- For a 10–40 person firm, the most transferable model is TopGear-style "real work as challenges": bounties tied to actual ServiceOS, School OS or logistics tickets, docs and tests, with small non-cash rewards. Simple badges and leaderboards (Wingspan-style) cover the learning layer.
- TCS's score-gated onboarding suggests interns could unlock tasks or levels as they demonstrate skills, which gives them a structured learning path.
- Because evidence is mostly vendor-claimed, the game should set its own baseline metrics (for example tickets closed, PR review turnaround, course completion) and measure before and after.

### Gaps
- No search results covered Tech Mahindra, Flipkart or Zoho gamification programs, and I found no TCS iON gamification results.
- Most sources are 2013–2019 trade press. I found no recent (2024–26) independent evaluations of these programs.

### Inferences
- **Seasonal arc:** a "season" per quarter with festival-themed events. Examples: a Pongal harvest event (tie to "harvesting" closed tickets) for a Tamil Nadu-based team; Holi colour-team mixers; IPL-season squad drafts; an Independence Day "Swadeshi build" hackathon; Onam pookalam-style collaborative boards; a Diwali "lights" season finale with awards just before the holiday. Diwali is also when employers traditionally give gifts, so prizes there are natural (watch the tax threshold in Section 5).
- **IPL/fantasy mental model:** an "auction" or draft of mixed intern/senior squads, captain/vice-captain multipliers for leads, "Orange/Purple Cap"-style category leaders, and points tables. All of this is free, with no entry fee or stake, to stay clearly outside "online money game".
- Respect religious and regional diversity: make festival events opt-in and inclusive, and rotate across regions (Pongal, Onam, Diwali, Eid, Christmas) rather than centring one tradition.

## 4. Intern programs: engagement, mentoring, fair competition with seniors, evaluation norms

### Takeaway
Internships in India are growing fast (+25% in 2024 per Internshala). Stipends vary widely (about ₹8k/month average per Internshala vs about ₹25k/month per Indeed). There is no central law on intern stipends. New hires and juniors are measurably under-recognised, so separate rookie tiers or categories are well justified. I found little India-specific data on mentoring or evaluation norms.

### Cited Findings
- Internship opportunities rose about 25% in 2024, with 135% growth since 2020 (Internshala Internship Trends Report 2024). — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/internshala-internship-trends-report-2024-25-percent-rise-in-internship-opportunities-observed-in-2024); [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/25-rise-in-internship-opportunities-observed-in-2024-internshala-internship-trends-report-2024/)
- Stipends: the Internshala 2024 average was ₹8,000/month, with premium tech/consulting offers up to ₹1,00,000. A 2025 Indeed analysis gives a ₹25,432/month national average, with Hyderabad, Bangalore, Mumbai, Pune and Gurgaon above average. The figures come from secondary coverage. — [The People's Board](https://www.thepeoplesboard.com/?p=23771); [Storyboard18 (Indeed)](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/internships-see-23-growth-in-india-as-companies-focus-on-future-talent-indeed-60318.htm)
- No central labour statute prohibits unpaid internships. Interns outside the Apprentices Act have no legal stipend benchmark. — [The People's Board](https://www.thepeoplesboard.com/?p=23771)
- Unpaid internships and undefined hours are a reported problem in emerging fields. — [Business Standard, Aug 2024](https://business-standard.com/industry/news/internship-woes-unpaid-opportunities-undefined-hours-hit-emerging-fields-124083000883_1.html)
- Students and corporates prefer 4–6 month internships (HirePro report). — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/most-students-corporates-prefer-4-6-month-internships-remote-work-college-placements-hirepro-report/amp)
- Almost half of Indian companies offering internships eventually hire the same candidates. — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/almost-half-the-companies-in-india-offering-internships-eventually-hire-the-same-candidates/articleshow/71607885.cms)
- Internshala's outlook expected mentorship and career coaching to gain prominence as intern benefits in 2025. This is a forecast, not survey data. — [Business News This Week](https://businessnewsthisweek.com/?p=280224)
- New hires and non-managerial staff are significantly under-recognised in India. — [GPTW India x Vantage Circle](https://www.greatplacetowork.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/the-recognition-effect-great-place-to-work-x-vantage-circle-1.pdf)
- Commentary says juniors in Indian IT are often expected to ramp up on unfamiliar stacks "without visibly asking". This is an assertion, not evidence. — [SHRM India](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/blogs/why-psychological-safety-in-india-requires-more-than-an-open-door-policy)

### Inferences
- **Rookie tier:** run a separate "Emerging/Rookie" leaderboard (like IPL's "Emerging Player" award) and normalise points by tenure or "improvement vs own baseline". Interns can then win without beating seniors on raw output.
- **Mentor pairing:** award points to seniors for mentoring actions (code reviews for interns, pairing sessions, answered questions). This aligns senior incentives with intern learning and reduces the face cost of interns asking.
- Many interns are on 2–6 month cycles and may become hires. Keep seasons short (about 6–8 weeks) so an intern experiences a full season and its awards. Game achievements can feed intern evaluation or PPO (pre-placement offer) conversations, but only if criteria are transparent and the scores are not the sole basis (see DPDP in Section 5).
- Prizes should matter but not dwarf stipends. A ₹2,000 voucher means much more to an intern on ₹8–15k/month than to a senior. Consider tier-appropriate prizes and experiences (lunch with founder, conference ticket, certificate, LinkedIn recommendation), which carry status value for students.

### Gaps
- I found no India-specific study on intern mentoring effectiveness or on typical intern evaluation rubrics (for example, how colleges grade internships, or AICTE internship credit requirements).
- I did not find data on interns competing alongside full-time staff in gamified settings.

## 5. Legal/policy considerations in India (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025; prizes and gift tax; Online Gaming Act 2025)

### Takeaway
As of 24 Nov 2026, the DPDP Rules 2025 are notified but most substantive duties (notice, security, rights, and Section 7 legitimate uses) apply only from about 13 May 2027. Building the game DPDP-ready now is prudent: give a clear notice, keep game data minimal, and get opt-in consent for anything beyond core employment purposes. The Online Gaming Act and Rules have been in force since 1 May 2026 and ban online money games, so the game must have no entry fees or stakes. Non-cash employer gifts are tax-free up to ₹15,000 per year under the new Income-tax Rules 2026 (from 1 April 2026); cash prizes are taxable salary.

### Cited Findings
**DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025**
- MeitY notified the DPDP Rules 2025 on 13/14 Nov 2025 (sources differ by a day; the PIB release says 14 Nov). They roll out in three phases over 18 months: procedural and Data Protection Board provisions immediately; Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) from about Nov 2026; most substantive obligations (Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23) from about 13/14 May 2027. — [PIB document](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/digital-personal-data-protection-dpdp-rules-2025-rollout-over-18-months); [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.in/en/insights/articles/dpdp-act-businesses-should-prepare-now-for-phased-obligations/)
- Section 7(i) ("legitimate uses") lets employers process employee data without consent for "purposes of employment". Commentators say it is not unrestricted and requires a proximate nexus to employment. Section 7 applies from 13 May 2027. — [Taxmann: Employee data and workplace monitoring](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/employee-data-workplace-monitoring-dpdp); [Bar & Bench (NovoJuris)](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/navigating-legitimate-use-exemption-employee-data-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-rely-on-the-employment-legitimate-use-under-section-7-i-of-the-dpdp-act)
- Taxmann says 7(i) does not license general surveillance, and every other Data Fiduciary duty (security, accuracy, erasure, grievance) still applies. Whether notice is required under 7(i) is debated. — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/employee-data-workplace-monitoring-dpdp); [Taxmann: Section 7 legitimate uses](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/legitimate-uses-section-7-dpdp-act)
- A union-oriented commentary (FES) argues for a narrow reading of 7(i) that would keep performance evaluation outside it. — [FES collections](https://collections.fes.de/publikationen/content/pagetext/1990055)
- Further analysis covers consent vs legitimate use for employee data. — [Mondaq: Is consent required to process employees' data](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1755320/is-consent-required-to-process-employees-personal-data-under-the-dpdp-act); [Baker McKenzie India handbook](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/asia-pacific/india/topics/data-processing-in-the-employment-context); [ELP Law PDF (May 2026)](https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Processing-employee-related-data-Where-are-the-potential-boundaries.pdf)

**Online Gaming Act 2025 (PROGA) and Rules 2026**
- The Act received Presidential assent on 22 Aug 2025. The government appointed 1 May 2026 as its commencement date. The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules 2026 were issued on 22 Apr 2026 and took effect on 1 May 2026. An earlier announced date of 1 Oct 2025 did not happen. The Online Gaming Authority of India is constituted under Section 8. — [SCC Online (Apr 2026)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/23/meity-notified-enforcement-of-promotion-regulation-online-gaming-act-2025/); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/online-gaming-rules-2026-to-be-effective-from-tomorrow/); [Mondaq: Act and Rules 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/india/social-media/1785870/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025-and-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-rules-2026); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/online-gaming-rules-bring-clarity-boost-esports-credibility-but-gaps-remain-say-industry-stakeholders-527052-2026-04-23)
- The Act's provisions have been challenged in court. I did not verify the current litigation status. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_and_Regulation_of_Online_Gaming_Act,_2025)
- **Definitions:**
  - An "online money game" is one where players pay fees, deposit money or other stakes expecting monetary or other enrichment, whether the outcome turns on skill, chance or both.
  - An "online social game" is offered solely for entertainment, recreation or skill development with no stake. A subscription or one-time access fee is allowed if it is not a stake.
  - E-sports are excluded from the money-game definition.
  - — [JGU Jindal Policy Research Lab](https://jgu.edu.in/jsgp/jindal-policy-research-lab/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025/); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act,-2025)
- Commentators note that "monetary gain" may extend to money-like rewards such as vouchers or trips, and recommend case-by-case review. — [Legal500: Social gaming doesn't escape regulation](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/indias-online-money-gaming-ban-how-social-gaming-gains-traction-but-doesnt-escape-regulation/)
- **Penalties:**
  - Offering an online money gaming service: up to 3 years' imprisonment and/or a ₹1 crore fine.
  - Advertising: up to 2 years and/or ₹50 lakh.
  - Repeat offences carry harsher penalties.
  - — [Taxmann analysis](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-ban-on-online-money-games-in-india-highlights-of-online-gaming-bill-2025); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/online-gaming-bill-2025-explained-what-s-allowed-what-s-banned-and-who-faces-penalties/article-179976)

**Tax on prizes and gifts to employees**
- The Income-tax Act 2025 and Income-tax Rules 2026 (notified 20 Mar 2026) replaced the 1961 Act and 1962 Rules from 1 April 2026. Employer non-cash gifts and vouchers now fall under Rule 15 and are tax-free up to ₹15,000 aggregate per financial year, up from ₹5,000 under the old Rule 3(7)(iv). Sources conflict on whether exactly ₹15,000 is inside or outside the limit. Crossing the threshold makes the whole amount taxable, not just the excess. All Diwali, New Year and festival gifts and vouchers in the year are aggregated. — [Khaitan & Co ERGO, Feb 2026](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-02/ERGO%20-%20Salary%20Taxation%20Revamped%20-%2027%20February%202026.pdf); [KPMG Flash Alert 2026-051](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-051.html); [CA Rajput: Diwali gifts under IT Act 2025](https://carajput.com/learn/diwali-gifts-under-i-t-act-2025-employee-gift-exemption-up-to-15k.html); [Vantage Circle: recognition tax rules India (Jul 2026)](https://www.vantagecircle.com/en/blog/recognition-rewards-tax-rules-india/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/new-tax-rules-2026-gifts-tax-free-upto-rs-15000-in-both-new-and-old-tax-regime-advantageclub-ai-ready-with-tax-saving-benefits-rewards/)
- Cash or cheque bonuses and prizes from an employer are fully taxable as salary from the first rupee. — [CA Rajput](https://carajput.com/learn/diwali-gifts-under-i-t-act-2025-employee-gift-exemption-up-to-15k.html); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/tax-on-diwali-and-new-year-gifts)
- Under the old regime, a gift card above the threshold was reported in full on Form 16. — [Tax Management India forum](https://taxmanagementindia.com/web/View_discussions_detail.asp?ID=119866)

### Inferences
**DPDP design**
- Treat game data (activity points, ticket-linked scores, quiz results) as personal data.
- Publish a short notice: what is tracked, why, who sees it, and how long it is kept.
- Keep data minimal. Pull aggregated counts from Jira, Git or other work tools rather than keystroke or screen-level monitoring.
- Make participation and public display opt-in (or allow opt-out of public leaderboards).
- Don't use game scores as the sole input to appraisals or intern hiring decisions. That stays outside a narrow reading of "employment purposes" and avoids a perception of surveillance.
- Delete or anonymise data at season end. Provide a contact for corrections or grievances.
- Interns who are not employees may not fall cleanly under 7(i), which makes explicit consent the safer basis for them.

**Online Gaming Act design**
- Charge no entry fee, take no stake, and offer no "buy-in" or paid power-ups. Never let players wager points convertible to money.
- Prizes should be awarded by the employer for work or learning achievements, not purchased chances.
- Avoid loot-box or random-draw mechanics tied to prizes. Pure-luck prize raffles may raise state gambling-law questions; I did not research this.
- An internal, free, recognition-oriented game looks like an "online social game" or not a "game service" at all, but legal confirmation is advisable.

**Prize budgeting**
- Keep each employee's total non-cash awards (game prizes plus Diwali gifts) under ₹15,000 per financial year to keep them tax-free. Track these per person.
- Avoid cash prizes, or route them through payroll with TDS.
- For interns (often not employees), different rules may apply. See Gaps.

### Gaps

## 6. Practical: mobile-first usage, WhatsApp/Google Workspace/Slack prevalence, bandwidth for remote players

### Takeaway
India is mobile-heavy (about 31–36 GB/month per user in 2025) and WhatsApp is near-universal for business communication. A lightweight, mobile-first web app with WhatsApp or chat notifications fits better than a heavy desktop or installed app. I found no data on Slack vs Google Workspace adoption in small Indian firms.

### Cited Findings
- **Data use:** Nokia MBiT puts Indian average monthly mobile data per user at over 31 GB in 2025 (27.5 GB in 2024), with 18% CAGR over five years; 5G carries about 47% of mobile broadband traffic. Ericsson cites about 36 GB/month per active smartphone user (late 2025). The methodologies differ. — [Deccan Herald (Nokia)](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/indias-average-monthly-mobile-data-usage-per-user-hits-31gb-in-2025-nokia-3950714); [Business Today (Ericsson)](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/india-dominates-global-mobile-data-usage-with-36-gb-per-month-503124-2025-11-21)
- **WhatsApp:** 91% of online adults in India talk to a business on WhatsApp (Kantar survey cited by Meta). India has more than 65 million SMBs. In a WhatsApp survey, 77% of Indian SMEs on WhatsApp said it helped them reach customers nationwide. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/12/whatsapp-brings-meta-verified-customized-messages-to-small-businesses-in-india); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=165002)
- Meta launched "Business AI on WhatsApp" for Indian small businesses in May 2026. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/introducing-business-ai-on-whatsapp-for-small-businesses/amp/)
- A user-count figure of 535M+ WhatsApp users in India appears only in a marketing blog and is unverified. — [Rasayel](https://learn.rasayel.io/en/blog/whatsapp-user-statistics/)

### Inferences
- **Build:** a mobile-first PWA (light pages, small images, offline-tolerant). Interactions should take 1–2 minutes, such as a daily quiz or kudos. Avoid large video or real-time 3D for remote players on patchy home broadband or mobile data.
- **Notifications:** post leaderboard updates and challenge prompts into the team's existing channel (WhatsApp group, Google Chat or Slack). Use Google Workspace sign-in if the company uses it.
- **WhatsApp caveats:** WhatsApp groups mix personal and work life. Keep pings infrequent and within working hours, and avoid posting full rankings there (face-saving). For bulk messaging, use the WhatsApp Business API rather than personal numbers.
- **Language:** keep core UI in English (the working language in Indian tech). Optional light Tamil/Hindi flavour in festival events, titles and badges (for example, Pongal "Sakkarai" badge names) can add warmth if the team is regionally concentrated. This is inference; I found no source.
